Super Smash Bros. Melee · Fox vs Fox · Final Destination

Perfect play, in five positions

What do two frame-perfect Foxes do to each other? We put the question to a solver running on the game's own code, where both players choose an input every frame, at the same time, each knowing only what happened up to the frame before. These are the positions it settled, replayed from the simulator one frame at a time (a frame is 1/60 of a second).

Like chess diagrams, each one is a small, exact situation. Blue is the attacker. Orange is the defender, standing with his back 12 units from the right ledge. Distances are between the two fighters' centres; a Fox is about 10 units tall.

  • shine, hitbox out (frame 1)
  • shine held, no hitbox
  • shield
  • intangible: cannot be hit
  • a hit lands
Position 1 · a draw

The standoff

Just outside each other's shine, neither Fox has a move that works. Anything one starts, the other sees a frame later and answers. The famous picture of this, two Foxes shining on the same frame, is the close-range version: it costs nobody anything either.

Position 2 · the measuring stick

How far the shine reaches depends on the target

The shine is the only move in the game with a hitbox on its first frame, so nobody can react to it. Its reach is short: about 10.7 units against a standing Fox. But a Fox who is crouching into a jump, or starting a roll, is hit from 12.6. The ticks under the stage mark both distances from blue. Those two extra units are what the next position turns on.

Position 3 · zugzwang

Shield is a one-way door

Orange is holding shield and blue stands 12.59 away, doing nothing. The shield is safe while it lasts, but it shrinks every frame, and there are six ways to leave it. Blue waits, sees which one orange picked a frame late, and punishes it. Frame 1 is the frame orange leaves. Five exits cost 5%; the sixth costs 18%.

Position 4 · a forced guess

The coin flip under a short hop

Blue is 9 frames into a short hop, 7.8 units away and 9.2 up, above the cornered orange. Suppose orange does not shield. Now nobody can wait and react: both have to choose on the same frame. Pick a cell to watch it.

Whichever move orange picks, one of blue's two moves hits him and the other does not, and the same is true the other way round. So the best either can do is flip a coin: each mixes half and half, and blue lands the first hit half of the time (game value 0.5, solved 8 frames deep). This is the first position where a perfect defender can be made to guess.

Position 5 · a mating net?UNPROVEN

The checkmate idea

Put positions 3 and 4 together. Hop at a cornered Fox: to avoid the coin flip he has to shield. Then land next to that shield, and it is the one-way door. If every step were forced, this would be a win from the corner. Every frame below is a real trace, but the line as a whole is a conjecture, not a result.

Measured

  • At hop frame 9, orange's only safe move is shield (position 4).
  • From where blue lands here, 6.8 units from the shield, five of the six exits are hit within 10 frames (checked with the solver for this exact position). The spotdodge outlasts that horizon, as in position 3.

Not shown

  • Orange stands still for the first 9 frames of the hop. Whether he can stop blue from getting there is unsolved.
  • Whether orange can leave the shield while blue is still falling or in landing lag. That search is running.
